What is Type 2 Diabetes?

A chronic condition where the body cannot use insulin effectively, causing blood glucose to rise — and why catching it early matters.

How it develops

In a healthy body, the hormone insulin moves glucose from the bloodstream into cells, where it fuels energy. In Type 2 diabetes, cells gradually stop responding to insulin (insulin resistance), and over time the pancreas can no longer compensate. Blood glucose stays persistently high, damaging blood vessels and nerves over years.

Type 2 diabetes accounts for more than 90 % of all diabetes cases worldwide, affecting an estimated 500 million adults — a number that continues to grow with ageing populations and changing diets.

Common signs

  • Increased thirst and frequent urination
  • Unusual tiredness and slow-healing wounds
  • Blurred vision
  • Often no obvious symptoms for years — which is why screening matters

Why early detection helps

Because Type 2 diabetes often develops silently, many people are diagnosed only after complications appear. Catching elevated risk early makes lifestyle changes and treatment far more effective — this is the gap a fast, non-invasive screening tool aims to help close.

How it is officially diagnosed

Clinical diagnosis relies on blood tests: fasting plasma glucose (≥126 mg/dL) or HbA1c (≥6.5 %). A saliva-based screening tool is not a replacement — it is a way to flag who might benefit from getting those tests.
