What is Type 2 Diabetes?

Type 2 diabetes is a long-term condition in which the body cannot use insulin effectively, so glucose builds up in the blood instead of fuelling cells.

How it develops

In a healthy body, the hormone insulin moves glucose from the bloodstream into cells. In Type 2 diabetes, cells gradually stop responding to insulin (insulin resistance), and over time the pancreas can no longer keep up. Blood glucose then stays persistently high, which damages blood vessels and nerves over the years.

It accounts for more than 90% of all diabetes cases worldwide and affects an estimated 500+ million adults, a number that continues to rise with ageing populations and changing diets.

Common symptoms

  • Increased thirst and frequent urination
  • Tiredness and slow-healing wounds
  • Blurred vision
  • Often, no obvious symptoms for years — which is why screening matters

Why early detection matters

Because Type 2 diabetes often develops silently, many people are diagnosed only after complications appear. Catching elevated risk early allows lifestyle changes and treatment that can slow or even reverse progression. This is the gap a fast, low-cost, non-invasive screening tool aims to help close.

How it is officially diagnosed

Clinical diagnosis relies on blood tests: fasting plasma glucose (≥126 mg/dL) or HbA1c (≥6.5%). A saliva-based screening tool like this one is not a replacement for those tests — it is a way to flag who might benefit from getting them.
