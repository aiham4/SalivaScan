Why Saliva? ATR-FTIR Spectroscopy Explained

Saliva carries a chemical snapshot of the body's metabolism. Unlike blood, it can be collected painlessly, anywhere, by anyone.

A non-invasive window into metabolism

Saliva contains glucose, proteins, lipids, and other molecules whose concentrations shift when metabolism changes — as happens in Type 2 diabetes. Because collection needs no needle, no clinician, and no special handling, it suits large-scale, low-cost screening well, especially where access to clinics or labs is limited.

What ATR-FTIR actually measures

ATR-FTIR (Attenuated Total Reflectance – Fourier Transform Infrared) spectroscopy shines infrared light at a sample. Different molecular bonds absorb specific infrared frequencies, producing a spectrum — a molecular fingerprint across the 399–4000 cm⁻¹ range. Diabetic and non-diabetic saliva differ subtly across this fingerprint in ways a trained model can learn to detect.

Why a neural network

The differences are spread across thousands of wavenumbers and are far too subtle to read by eye. A neural network trained on labelled spectra picks up the combined pattern and turns a full spectrum into a single risk estimate.

Honest limitations

  • Saliva composition is affected by diet, hydration, time of day, and oral health.
  • The model was trained on one dataset (SEDENA, Mexico) and may not generalise to other populations or instruments.
  • This is a screening aid, not a diagnosis — positive results should be confirmed with a blood test.
